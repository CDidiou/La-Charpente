# The Framework (A Novel from the Bernard-Lilaz Cycle)

## A Note on the Author
Philip de Bernard-Lilaz, known as SciFi
![Photo by the author](/La-Charpente/images/JosephWw1.png)

Born in 1876 in a hamlet no map ever thought worth naming in full, the youngest of a family too large for the land that was meant to feed it, he left France for Quebec at the age of fifteen. He spent years felling trees in forests larger than anything he had ever known, before returning in 1916.

A few months later, the Somme took one of his legs.

When he came home, he realised there was little left for him to return to. So he began to search. First for a place, then for land, then for traces. He started going through papers, memories, half-told stories. He wanted to know what had been there before him, what belonged to him, what he might in turn leave behind. The more he searched, the more he came to believe that somewhere there was an older inheritance, something greater, almost legendary. A treasure. Gold coins, perhaps.

He spent his life looking for them.

He bought pieces of forest nobody wanted, felled his own timber by hand, built a house. He carried on with one leg missing and the stubbornness of a man who had decided to owe nothing to chance. He gathered whatever could be gathered. He built whatever could be made to stand.

But a house is not a story.

Late in life, almost at the end, he wrote a single book. Four characters search for what we receive from the dead, what we pass on to the living, what we repeat without ever knowing why. They speak around what matters most, as families do when they have waited too long.

The book found neither publisher nor readers.

He died with his house, his land and his gold coins — if they ever existed.

A century later, someone would find his book in an attic. And perhaps understand then what he himself had spent a lifetime trying to understand: he had not been looking for a treasure. He had been looking for a story.


## The Book

The Framework is a family novel.

There is what a family says around the table, out loud, between courses. And then there is everything else — what passes between them without ever being named, what is handed down without anyone choosing it, what gets repeated because no one before them ever took the time to say it aloud.

A father who holds the table together without being asked. A mother who forges connections with disconcerting ease. One son who charges ahead so he never has to wait. Another who understands everything except how to let himself be touched. Four ways of loving, four ways of protecting oneself from loving too much.

The Framework tells of what holds a family upright without being seen — care and connection, those fibres woven into the very grain of the wood, giving the whole structure its strength. And what those two forces can bring forth, especially once they stop exhausting themselves by carrying everything alone: a trust broad enough to venture where they never knew they could go.

A novel in dialogue, a framework dismantled and rebuilt before our eyes — because, in the end, all we ever have is raw timber and examples. The rest we shape for ourselves, move around, take up again awkwardly, often in ways entirely contrary to what we thought we were doing. And it is that construction — part innate, part acquired — that the wind will reveal.

We do not choose the framework beneath which we are born. But each of us chooses, without always knowing it, what we add to it.


## Acknowledgments

I have no one to thank for this book. I came back from Canada expressly to fight for this country, for this family, for that dream. I fought, and the dream slipped away all the same. I worked the wood, invested in plots of land nobody wanted, and I never managed, myself, to build a real home.

I give away everything that remains, provided it sees me through my old age — and along with it, I give my lessons in life, so that others may try again, and achieve what I did not know how to do.

A framework is only of any use if it shelters a warm home.

**Family, love one another.**
