*(One autumn evening. The living room has gradually emptied towards the kitchen and the coffee. The governess of the Elder Son’s children, who has been with them for more than ten years now and has become a full-fledged member of the household, remains sitting alone by the window, a barely touched plate on her lap, her gaze elsewhere. The Elder Son is the first to notice her.)*

**The Elder Son :**
You okay?

**The governess :**
Yeah, yeah.

**The Elder Son :**
You barely ate anything.

**The governess :**
I’m not very hungry, that’s all.

*(The Elder Son hesitates, then does what he knows how to do.)*

**The Elder Son :**
Want me to pour you some more coffee? It’s still hot.

**The governess :**
No, thanks.

*(The Elder Son remains standing for a moment, not knowing what to do with his hands for the first time that evening. The Younger Son, passing by with two cups, stops.)*

**The Younger Son :** *quietly, to his brother*
Leave her alone.

**The Elder Son :**
I’m just offering her some coffee.

**The Younger Son :**
I know. Leave her alone anyway.

*(The Elder Son steps aside, a little piqued, and heads back towards the kitchen. The Younger Son sits down beside the Governess without a word. He doesn’t put the cups down right away. He doesn’t ask what’s wrong. A long silence. The Governess continues to stare out the window.)*

**The governess :**
It’s hard, isn’t it?

**The Younger Son :**
What is?

**The governess :**
Nothing. One more year. I was looking at the table earlier. There are empty chairs this year.

**The Younger Son :**
Yeah.

*(He says nothing more. No words about grief, no comment about a passing generation, nothing one would normally expect from him. Just silence, sitting beside her.)*

**The governess :**
Nothing to say for once?

**The Younger Son :**
No.

**The governess :** *(with a little sad laugh)*
Well, would you look at that.

*(The Elder Son returns with the coffee, stops at a distance when he sees the scene, and doesn’t dare approach. He looks at his brother, who still hasn’t said anything, who still hasn’t done anything — and yet, apparently, has achieved something that he hasn’t.)*

**The governess :**
Stay here a little, will you?

**The Younger Son :**
I’m not going anywhere.

*(Another silence, lighter this time. The Governess pats the Younger Son on the shoulder. They smile at each other. Later, in the kitchen, the Elder Son puts the cups down, annoyed without really knowing why.)*

**The Elder Son :**
How did you do that?

**The Younger Son :**
I didn’t do anything.

**The Elder Son :**
Exactly.

**The Younger Son :**
Maybe that’s the point.

**The Elder Son :**
I offered her coffee. I even almost suggested going for a walk. I tried everything.

**The Younger Son :**
You tried to fix something that just needs to be looked at.

**The Elder Son :**
And you just sat down.

**The Younger Son :**
Yeah.

**The Elder Son :**
This drives me crazy. Usually, you’re the one who explains everything and I’m the one who does things. This time it was the other way around, and I don’t even know what you did.

**The Younger Son :**
I didn’t explain anything to anyone. Not even to myself. I just… didn’t need to understand why she was sad. I felt it, that’s all!

*(The Elder Son looks at him, almost suspiciously, as though searching for the theory hidden behind the words.)*

**The Elder Son :**
Are you explaining something to me right now, or not?

**The Younger Son :** *(smiling)*
I don’t know. You’re the one who can tell straight away whether something works or not — it worked, didn’t it?

**The Elder Son :**
Yeah. It worked.

**The Younger Son :**
Then that’s it. We don’t need to know why.

*(The Elder Son keeps looking at him, almost incredulous to hear him say that — he who always explains why.)*

**The Elder Son :**
This is the first time I’ve ever heard you say something without explaining it afterwards.

*(They look at each other for a second, both almost surprised, then the Elder Son grabs the two remaining cups of coffee.)*

**The Elder Son :**
Right. I’ll take this to her, then. Without offering anything.

**The Younger Son :**
Go ahead.

**The Elder Son :**
You’ll tell me if I did the right thing.

**The Younger Son :**
No.

**The Elder Son :** *(leaving, half amused)*
Okay.

*(The Governess stares at him. She gets to her feet, fists on her hips.)*

**The governess :**
Oh! Are you going to stop pacing around with that coffee? You’ve been walking around with it for ten minutes! If it’s lukewarm water you want to drink, there was no point in making a whole pot of coffee!

*(The Elder Son stands speechless, cup in hand, completely caught off guard. Off to the side, the Younger Son wears his little analyst’s smile, ready to make some pointed remark about silent resilience.)*

**The governess :**
And you, laughing over there! Can’t you hear there hasn’t been any music for ages? Good grief, we’re bored here! He plays us these tearjerking songs, and then when the song ends, nada, nobody moves! Come on, don’t worry, I’ll take care of it. We’re going to have some fun!
