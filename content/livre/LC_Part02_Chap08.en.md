*(The father's childhood home, in the middle of construction. The walls are bare, dust covers everything, a thermos of coffee placed on a work surface that no longer exists. The parents are keeping the grandchildren for the holidays, and have let them play in the next room while the two of them take a breather for a moment, sitting on two upturned buckets.)*

**The mother:**
Did you see how your son left you sitting the other day? He took the chairs from your hands, texting you, “You don’t have to be the one to hold everything. »

**The father:**
Yeah. And you, with the hedge? He stopped you from watching the house by cutting two crossbar branches.

**The mother:**
“Just look at the hedge, Mom. » It feels like an internship.

**The father:**
An internship of what, note?

**The mother:**
I don't know. Something like “full presence and mindful gardening”. One hundred and eighty euros a day, with herbal tea at noon.

*(The father almost chokes on laughter in his thermos cup.)*

**The father:**
Stop, you'll make me spit out my coffee. And the silence, with your son, then? Do you know what he told me? “We don’t need to know why. » For a moment I thought he was going to offer me a rain stick.

**The mother:**
And the table plan! He gave me three constraints on a piece of paper like a consultant who bills by the hour. “One thing at a time, Mom. »

**The father:**
Our sons have become life coaches without anyone warning us.

*(They laugh frankly, that real mad laugh that starts again just when you think it's stopping. A crash of hurried footsteps, screams, a door slamming against the wall. The five little ones burst into the room, excited, one of them already brandishing a piece of pipe found somewhere.)*

**The grandson:**
Grandpa, can we help?? There are still walls to break down??

*(The father exchanges a look with the mother. A big smile appears on his face.)*

**The father:**
There's some left. A lot, even.

*(He gets up, fetches three sledgehammers, two sledgehammers, and a mallet from a corner, and distributes them without the slightest hesitation, without helmet, without glasses, without a safety word.)*

**The father:**
This one doesn't hold anything anymore anyway. You can tap on it all you want.

*(It's immediately chaos. The children rush to the partition wall in the corridor, hit randomly, laughing loudly, dust in their hair, one screams with joy at each blow, another hits squarely next to the wall and misses everything, without anyone taking offense.)*

**The mother:** *(yelling over the noise)*
Aim for the bottom of the wall, not the top, there is an electrical conduit!

**The father:**
Cut since this morning, don't worry!

*(An entire section of partition collapses in a cloud of plaster, followed by a collective cry of victory. The mother sits back on her bucket, laughing, sipping her coffee while the construction site turns into a real fair. He sits next to her, looks at her for a moment, grabs her by the waist, and kisses her, without hurrying, while the children continue to demolish happily behind them.)*

**The mother:**
You realize. Our two learned sons who gave us a whole speech on letting go, on letting things flow, and carrying everything on their own.

**The father:**
And in the end, who is the one who fixes the house?

**The mother:**
Us.

**The father:**
And who looks after the kids during the holidays, while Mister negotiates his deals and Mister analyzes the market?

**The mother:**
Us again.

*(They both burst out laughing, a laugh that mixes with the noise of the hammers and the cries of the children.)*

**The father:**
They have great theories, our sons. But the reality is that it has continued to be run by the same little hands for forty years.

**The mother:**
So much the better. I wouldn't have known what to do with a peaceful retirement anyway.

*(One of the grandchildren appears in front of them, covered from head to toe in white dust, a huge smile on his face.)*

**The grandson:**
Grandpa, grandma, can we break down another wall?

**The father:**
Go ahead, big guy. As long as it's left, break whatever you want.

*(He rests his head against the mother's shoulder for a moment, amidst the noise and dust, visibly happier than he has been in a long time.)*
