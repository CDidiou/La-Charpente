## The father

**Which he wears well.** Warm, he knows how to make everyone feel like they have their place, without even having to ask for it. It's a rare and almost invisible gift: no one notices the work it requires, because it seems so natural to him. He runs the table, literally and symbolically – the one at home, the one on Sunday, the one that stays set no matter what happens. This isn't passivity disguised as kindness — it's a form of power that never seeks attention. He never builds to isolate himself, he builds so that others have a place to come back to. He is good-natured, expansive, the type to fill a room with his voice and his laughter without it weighing on anyone. His hands know what they're doing: a creaky floor, a wall that's not straight, he corrects them in a second, almost without thinking, with a quiet pleasure in the gesture itself - more skillful, in fact, than either of his two sons, who inherited everything but that. The world, for him, has simple codes: a thing is well done or it is not, a line is straight or it is not. He has never had a taste for the tortuous paths of his younger brother nor for the rapid and sometimes destructive paths of his elder - what he seeks, above all else, is harmony.

**Which weakens him.** He grew up trying to repair, even as a child, a couple that was not his to repair - his own father, conciliatory but withdrawn in the home, expansive and festive whenever he was outside; his own mother, who had the reins of the house and actively ran the household. This habit of smoothing, of softening, of making sure that no one feels too much, he does not always know where the service rendered ends and where the forgetting of himself begins. It took a son watching him renovate a house for him to dare say, for the first time, that he too was afraid of not having anything solid to pass on.

**His injury.** Not being the one who builds big things, in a family that only talks about that. “I was just holding the table,” he says of himself, with a false modesty that hides a real worry: what if holding the table wasn’t enough?

**Which can be annoying about him.** The way he minimizes what he does ("I don't hold anything, I do things") at the precise moment when we try to thank him - as if he always has to refuse the compliment before we have even finished saying it. And this old tendency to give in too quickly, to swallow a frustration rather than say it right away, even if it means bringing it out intact, thirty years later, with all its preserved bitterness. His search for harmony, too, can turn into avoidance: he quickly tires of the younger brother's detours and bristles at the elder's brutality, as if all complexity or friction was, in principle, something poorly done.

## The mother

**What she wears well.** A force of decision that has held together a couple, a career, two sons, and probably half of the people she has encountered in her life. She knows how to create a bond quickly, strongly, with a rare intensity — in a classroom, in a retirement home corridor, in a team. She also has, which is even rarer, the ability to question herself publicly, in front of her own children, without it crushing her: she can hear "you took the knowledge, not the courage" without defending herself, and turn it into clarity rather than injury.

**Which weakens her.** Her strength itself, turned against her: she has difficulty leaving space without even realizing it, deciding for others when she thinks she is protecting them. It opens the links with disconcerting ease, but always on the same side of the scales - the one who heals, never the one who could become an issue again, or worse, be left behind.

**Her wound.** Having been, as a child, an issue rather than a person loved for her own sake — useful in preventing a couple from tearing themselves apart, rather than chosen unconditionally. She spent her life returning this wound with force, without always seeing that she continued, sometimes, to pay the price elsewhere, on other ties, other generations.

**Which can be annoying about her.** The way she decides before someone has finished asking the question, arriving on a construction site and redistributing roles in thirty seconds without consulting anyone. And this way, still today, of closing a subject a little too quickly as soon as she feels that it is settled for her - even if, for the other person opposite, this is not yet quite the case.

## The eldest son

**Which he wears well.** Momentum. He doesn't know the fear of starting: a business, a team, a relationship, he goes for it, and people follow him. He inherited from his mother this ability to create movement around him, and he transformed it into an entrepreneurial career - tech, finance: modern territories to conquer, more than materials to shape. He is capable of his hands when the stakes merit it - a wall to straighten, a steak never to be missed, thirty years without a fault - but it is never the gesture that nourishes him, it is the victory that it represents. Unlike his father, who remained anchored in the concrete of a generation that worked earth and wood, he prefers the challenges that are played out on a screen, a market, a stage - less dirt under his fingernails, more territories to dominate. He also knows, which is rarer in such a temperament, to recognize that he is wrong - in front of his father, in front of his mother, in front of his brother - without it damaging him.

**Which weakens him.** He has built his entire life in reaction against the image of an overly conciliatory father, and discovers, once he becomes a father in turn, that his own sons do not all follow him with the same enthusiasm - what he calls, with a touch of sad irony, "dad syndrome". He transmits, without meaning to, an anger born from an old feeling of betrayal, over trifles that are not really trifles.

**His hurt.** The fear of having been right against his father for the wrong reasons — of having chosen speed and firmness as a response to a childhood that he may have misread as a child.

**Which can be annoying about him.** His impatience, his way of deciding for others before asking their opinion - exactly the gesture that he sometimes reproaches his mother for, without always seeing it in himself at the same moment. And this propensity to despise, even affectionately, his brother's slowness, as if taking his time was a weakness rather than another strength.

## The youngest son

**Which he wears well.** A look of rare finesse, inherited directly from his mother: he sees, in a meeting, in a family, in a sentence, what no one else sees. He knows how to name with almost surgical precision what others feel without being able to say it - "you gave me the keys to a house, and forbade me to open the door" is a sentence that only he could formulate.

**Which weakens him.** He grew up overprotected, pampered like the daughter his mother would have liked to have, and because of that, never had the opportunity to develop the muscles that you gain by hitting yourself. He grew up mostly as the object of his mother's care, rather than as her equal—loved, protected, but rarely encountered as he truly was. He learned, without meaning to, a silent lesson: that proximity is always accompanied by control, on one side or the other. So he prefers to observe the links before risking them - to keep the position of the one who understands, rather than that of the one who could, in turn, be managed as a case. Which makes him brilliant at reading people, and cautious to the point of excess when relating to them.

**His injury.** Never having had the right to exercise, for himself, the gift that was nevertheless transmitted to him in its entirety - decoding, intuition, psychology. He took the knowledge without the courage, and he knows it, and it continues to work on him.

**What can be annoying about him.** His tendency to intellectualize everything, including his own emotions, including moments when he should just feel without commenting. And this slowness in deciding which, seen from the outside, can pass for indecision whereas, for him, it is a real method - but a method which wears out the patience of those who love him and who wait, sometimes too long, for him to get started.
