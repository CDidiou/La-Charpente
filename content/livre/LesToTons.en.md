# Imaginary dialogue between Brothers

---

**The eldest son:**
You know, I was thinking about our family tree the other day. To everything we wear without even realizing it. Do you want to watch this together?

**The youngest son:**
Gladly. I've always loved mapping things. Begin.

**The eldest son:**
So. On Dad's side, there was a grandfather in tune with the collective hive, a manual man, who constantly sought harmony in the face of a grandmother who had to run daily life. Dad grew up there, and he took on exactly the same costume: celebration, warmth, generosity, but with an underlying melancholy, and this visceral need for everyone to get along well.

**The youngest son:**
And on mom's side?

**The eldest son:**
The type factory! Grandpa? A real chef's temperament! Grandma? An iron structure with velvet around it, it has counterattacked all its life. Mom grew up in that house, she toughened up there.

**The youngest son:**
Stories of lions and conciliators. Note, we talk about it easily, with our words today. They didn't have this luxury. It was after the war. You had to feed the house, not wonder where your anger came from.

**The eldest son:**
It's true. We stick labels on them a posteriori, with the comfort of having time. They didn't even have the words for that, or even the right to use them.

**The youngest son:**
Times were tough.

**The eldest son:**
Exactly. And I think that's why mom and dad chose each other. She needed a gentle man to prove that you could reign without crushing. He needed to tame a strong figure to somehow seduce his mother!

**The youngest son:**
A cross repair. Each replaying, through the other, a story older than their relationship.

**The eldest son:**
There you go. And us in all this?

**The youngest son:**
We were born at the crossroads of these two lineages. You have adopted mom's habit: the command, the action, the desire to build quickly and big. I took a tangent. Faced with a house where strong characters sometimes clashed, I chose height. I observe, I analyze, I take a step back before acting. It's my way of not entering the arena.

**The eldest son:**
You've always been like that, even when you were little. While I rushed, you watched.

**The youngest son:**
I was mapping. And you, you have never been afraid to move forward. It's a strength that I don't have. I have the vision but not the leverage!

**The eldest son:**
Let's talk about our life choices, then. I built quickly: I wanted to build something big, take risks, move forward without looking back too much. You took the time. You thought for a long time before taking the plunge, explain your choices to me.

**The youngest son:**
I need to understand. You prefer to understand while walking. But when I walk, I can no longer understand so well. So I don't move! And I see.

**The eldest son:**
And our children, in all this, what will they inherit from us?

**The youngest son:**
Here you have to be honest. We don't just transmit strengths.

**The eldest son:**
Go ahead and say it.

**The youngest son:**
Let me put this in order, because it's been running around in my head for a while but I haven't been able to put it clearly. I think I passed on my fear. I cooled energy and curiosity into prudence, analysis, observation.

**The eldest son:**
Are you sure it was you who did this? Or are they just like you?

**The youngest son:**
Good question. I don't know yet where the limit is, really. What I know is that I pay close attention to the wounds we inflict on them despite ourselves, and to what they see of our relationship. And despite that, on balance, they still see clearly. They detect that I am paying attention to showing them a theater. That makes them very keen observers. But a keen observer is already someone who has learned to be wary of what is shown to him.

**The eldest son:**
If you convey your fear, then I convey my anger, I guess. Except... no, wait, that's not even it. Yet I don't see myself like you, in a mirror effect. I'm not a pure go-getter like I was.

**The youngest son:**
How's that?**The eldest son:**
I'm experiencing daddy syndrome. I see children who don't look like me. The other evening, I saw myself getting angry again, nothing serious, over a trifle, a last minute cancellation. I heard myself raise my voice. Afterwards I said to myself: it wasn't the cancellation that had that effect on me, it was the feeling of having been pushed aside without being told to my face. I reacted as if I had been betrayed, even though he is only young and he was too lazy to go to this thing.

**The youngest son:**
Ultimately, through our own children, we discover ourselves. I finally see clearly my insecurity, my refusal to connect with others, my fear of being judged. I have carried this dizziness all my life without naming it, and now I see it in the mirror. Worse, I think I'm passing it on.

**The eldest son:**
I'm not sure it's as clear cut as you say for you. Insecurity can also just be caution, right? You may be finding a wound where there is only a temperament.

**The youngest son:**
Maybe. Or maybe temperament is precisely what we build over the wound so that we no longer have to look at it. I don't know how to decide right away.

**The eldest son:**
I see something else, in any case, at home. That old feeling that you can be let go just when you least expect it. I've never said it like that to anyone, but I see it coming out in my sons in the form of anger. An anger that builds quickly, over nothing, as soon as they feel that we are not keeping our word, or that we are letting them down.

**The youngest son:**
You betrayal, me insecurity, then. We passed on our qualities, yes, but also the wound that gave birth to them in the first place.

**The eldest son:**
Dogs don't make cats. But they don't make exactly the same dog again.
The dynamics play out on several axes. Each child will also instinctively look for empty places. As for both of us, I took one place, you took another!

**The youngest son:**
The strength of parents is to transmit a lessened wound. We build ourselves on wounds. But we collapse on traumas.

**The eldest son:**
Hey, note, we are doing exactly what we were saying earlier. We dissect our moods on a Sunday afternoon, quietly. Dad, at our age, he didn't have that luxury.

**The youngest son:**
Neither him nor mom. Even less their parents. They didn't have time to ask themselves why they were afraid or why they were angry. They just had to hold on, keep the house running, feed everyone.

**The eldest son:**
We have the right to do this, you and I, only because they didn't have it. It's almost an indecent privilege, this conversation.

**The youngest son:**
Indecent, no. But a privilege, surely. They spared us enough of the harshness that we had time to look back instead of just surviving ahead. And they prepared the ground, opened the analysis.

**The eldest son:**
Mom and Dad were truly good parents.

**The youngest son:**
Imagine the violence of life, of situations, of precariousness, and the cocoon that we had! Hat !

**The eldest son:**
Tell me, you who like to think in the long term: could we, one day, do something together, you and I? Our two branches, mine which rushes and yours which thinks?

**The youngest son:**
I've already thought about it, actually. I think we would make a formidable team if we agreed not to step on each other's toes. You and your wife are made for deployment: action, negotiation, large-scale implementation. My wife and I are made for upstream: the idea, the study, the anticipation of risks, the solidity of the foundations, the recurring analysis.

**The eldest son:**
Difficult to articulate. There is potential, but such a different time.
I am annoyed by your slowness, you despise my haste.

**The youngest son:**
And dad's house. This is the project that awaits. We have to fix it, all of us, all three of us, with Dad!

**The eldest son:**
First you need a craftsman. Afterwards we can play “you think, I act”. 

**The youngest son:**
Do you think we should take a vacation where we exchange our children for a few weeks? Like a rapprochement through intermediary children.

**The eldest son:**
It's funny, well. It would be a tasty double blend, where each culture infuses, each child captures a different life dynamic.

**The youngest son:**
And above all, it would put them each in a place that does not exist within their own siblings. At home, they would find themselves slowly deciphering the great map of the world. At home, he would no longer have the luxury of remaining withdrawn from the world.**The eldest son:**
They would go try to occupy another space.
But hey, they're starting to get a little big for this kind of game.

**The youngest son:**
It doesn't matter. In the middle of all this, there is always the pleasure of meeting again.

**The eldest son:**
Thanks to Mom and Dad!

**The youngest son:**
Dad. Neither of us really resembles him, neither in temperament nor in choices. He has this generosity and this true joy that holds the whole building together!

**The eldest son:**
Mom. She gave us this strength that we both have, each in our own way. Without her, her finesse in sublimating authoritarianism, neither you nor I would have had the courage to move forward, one by charging forward, the other by digging.

**The youngest son:**
And the two of us, with all that. I think we ended up forging a shield, each our own, with what we received from them. You in action, me in observation. It protected us, I'm sure. It may have also locked us in a bit, without us realizing it.

**The eldest son:**
A shield. It's a big word.

**The youngest son:**
Maybe. But it's the one that comes to me. We both had to build something solid to stand up in a house where there was so much love and so much character at the same time.

**The eldest son:**
Yes, we owe them a lot.
And now it's up to us to make the rest easy to carry!
