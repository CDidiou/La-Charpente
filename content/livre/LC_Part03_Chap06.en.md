The house has been under seal for eight days.  
The new shutters remain closed. The “Bed and Breakfast” sign has disappeared.

The eldest arrives at the younger's house one Tuesday morning, a file under his arm.  
He doesn't take off his coat.

**Tazar:**
I filed a motion to cancel the seizure.  
There are irregularities in the procedure. Deadlines were not met. We can still bring it down.

---

The lawyer's office smells of new leather and strong coffee. The eldest reserved the small back room, the one called the “conciliation room”. Three armchairs, a coffee table, a window overlooking a gray interior courtyard. The lawyer left them alone, saying he would come back when they needed him to formalize.

The eldest has already opened his file. Neatly aligned sheets, post-its, pen ready.

**Tazar:**
We need to sort this out today. The bank won't wait. If we transfer the shares now, we can still negotiate a staggering. You sign here, and here. Then we see about the house.

*(The youngest has not touched the documents. He looks at the table, then the window, then his brother.)*

**2D:**
You have already decided everything.

**Tazar:**
I have everything prepared. It's not the same thing.  
If we don't move, they seize. Do you want us to lose everything at once?

*(Pause. The eldest glances at the door, makes sure it is closed, leans a little, his voice lowered.)*

**Tazar:**
We really have to go quickly. Faster than what I'm telling you.

**2D:**
Why quickly like that, precisely.

*(The eldest hesitates. It’s not a strategic hesitation this time — something different, more naked.)*

**Tazar:**
My box is taking on water.

**2D:**
Since when?

**Tazar:**
For a while. Since before the accident, in fact. The accident just... sped things up. Two customers left at the same time last month. The supplier threatens to cut deliveries if I don't pay within three weeks. I don't have much left in front of me.

*(The cadet remains silent, time to understand.)*

**2D:**
And the house, in all that.

**Tazar:**
The house is for dad. Officially. But if I can generate some cash from the sale, part of it will also be useful to me. To last a few more months. Time to turn things around.

**2D:** 
When were you planning to tell me?

**Tazar:**
There. NOW. Before mom comes.

*(He looks his brother in the face, for the first time since he entered the room.)*

**Tazar:**
Not a word to her. Nor to dad. They've already carried enough. If mom finds out I'm leaking too, she's going to want to sort it out herself, as usual, and I refuse. That's my problem, not his.

**2D:** 
And I'm supposed to carry this on my own, on top of everything else.

**Tazar:**
But you have nothing to wear. You just have to sign.

*(A silence. The cadet looks at the papers spread out on the table, differently now.)*

**2D:**
I understood. It's okay.

**Tazar:** *(a nod, a look)*
Thank you, brother.

*(He straightens up, immediately closes his face, resumes his usual tone, harsher, as if the confidence had never taken place.)*

**Tazar:**
Good. The signatures, then.

*(The door opens. The mother enters without knocking. She carries a thick binder under her arm, the same one she once used for her school files. She places it on the table, next to that of the eldest, and sits down. She does not take off her coat.)*

**The mother:**
Hello.

*(The two sons are silent.)*

**Tazar:**
Mom, we were...

**The mother:**
I know.  
I collected everything.

*(She opens the binder. Inside, folders labeled by hand: *Accounts – Insurance – Medical – Home – Business – Contacts*. Each document is classified, annotated in pencil, dated.)*

**The mother:**
Access codes. The proxies I still had. The latest readings. The name of the notary. That of the insurer. Doctors' numbers. What I was able to recover from your company's debts.  
It's all there. You won't have to search for anything.

*(The eldest leans over, leafs through quickly. The younger one does not move.)*

**2D:**
You did this alone.

**The mother:**
Yes.

**Tazar:**
That's good. We will be able to move forward faster. You just need to sign this page again, and have him sign the transfer.

*(The mother closes the binder with a clean gesture and pushes it towards the middle of the table, between the two of them.)*

**The mother:**
No.  
I don't sign anything anymore.  
I don't decide anything anymore.  
I don't hold anything anymore.

*(Silence.)*

**Tazar:**
Mom, we need...

**The mother:**
You don't need me anymore.  
It's all in the binder. You have the papers, you have the keys, you have the numbers.  
It's yours now.

*(She gets up.)*

**2D:**
You can't just...

**The mother:**
Yes.  
I can.*(She adjusts her coat.)*

**Tazar:**
And dad?

**The mother:**
Your father is talking about the workbench. Graffiti. Meals from before.  
He is already elsewhere.  
Me too, now.

*(She crosses the room. At the door, she turns around one last time. Not to beg them, not to apologize. Just to check that they understood correctly.)*

**The mother:**
Don't call me for details.  
I won't answer anymore.  
Get on with it.

*(She leaves. The door closes quietly.)*
*(The two brothers remain motionless. The file cabinet is there, between them, closed. The eldest looks at it like a construction site that must be tackled immediately. The younger looks at it like an object that we no longer know where to put.)*

**Tazar:**
Good.  
We start with the transfer.

**2D:**
Did you hear what she said?

**Tazar:**
Yes.  
She gave us everything. So we do what we need to do.

*(He already opens the first folder. The youngest has still not touched the papers. He looks at the closed door, then the window, then his brother.)*

**2D:**
She cut.

**Tazar:**
She transmitted.  
It's not the same.

**2D:**  
Yes.  
It's exactly the same.

*(The eldest does not answer. He has already taken out his pen.)*
