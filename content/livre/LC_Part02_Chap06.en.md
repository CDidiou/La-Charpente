*(Later, the meal is over, everyone has gone into the living room. A silence settles between the conversations - not awkward, just a natural hollowness. The father gets up, uneasy, already looking for an idea to start something up again. The younger brother holds him by the sleeve, discreetly.)*

**The youngest son:**
Sit down.

**The father:**
Nothing more happens there. I'm going to get a digestive.

**The youngest son:**
There is silence. It's not the same as nothing.

**The father:**
How so.

**The youngest son:**
Look at them. Nobody looks bad. Your friend is dozing, the little couple of neighbors, they hold hands without speaking, the children play alone on the floor. Nobody expects you to do anything.

*(The father sits back, not totally convinced, his eyes still checking.)*

**The father:**
I always have the impression that if I do nothing, we will no longer value each other.

**The youngest son:**
I know. But look what just happened right now. Nobody fell apart. The silence just lasted, and nothing fell.

**The father:**
Maybe it holds because I'm here anyway, ready to intervene if necessary.

**The youngest son:**
Yes, maybe. You just have to wait to check then!.

*(The father looks at him, almost stung.)*

**The father:**
You think I don't know how to stop.

**The youngest son:**
I don't think you've ever tested it, to see if it collapses without your donations. Because you never stop long enough to see.

*(A silence between them, this time. The father looks around the room again, but differently — not to assess whether it holds up, just to watch.)*

**The father:**
It's strange just looking.

**The youngest son:**
Let's listen to the sound of links. You know how to do it so well. But let's listen to everything. Not what seems to be missing. Just what there is.

*(The father remains silent, really looks this time: his friend sleeping, the couple looks at the children on the ground, the youngest next to him.)*

**The father:**
There's nothing missing, actually.

**The youngest son:**
There you go.

**The father:**
So what am I for?

*(The cadet doesn't respond right away — no theory ready, for once, just time to find something true.)*

**The youngest son:**
You're not there to hold things together. You need to be there while it lasts. It's not nothing, Dad. It's even rarer than the other thing.

*(The father says nothing, but something in his shoulders relaxes, a little. The younger one massages his painful shoulder for a second, and falls silent — for real, this time, without preparing anything to say next.)*
