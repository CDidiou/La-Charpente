# What you can't repair with your hands

---

*An autumn evening. The living room gradually emptied towards the kitchen and the café. The governess of the eldest's children, with them for more than ten years now, having become a full member of the house, remains sitting alone near the window, a plate barely touched on her knees, looking elsewhere. The eldest notices her first.*

**The eldest son:**
Are you okay?

**The housekeeper:**
Yes yes.

**The eldest son:**
You barely ate anything.

**The housekeeper:**
I'm not very hungry, that's all.

*The eldest hesitates, then does what he knows how to do.*

**The eldest son:**
Shall I pour some coffee for you? It's still hot

**The housekeeper:**
No, that's nice.

*The eldest stands for a moment, not knowing what to do with his hands for the first time that evening. The youngest, who was passing with two cups, stops.*

**The youngest son, low, to his brother:**
Leave her.

**The eldest son:**
I'm just offering a coffee.

**The youngest son:**
I know. Leave her anyway.

*The eldest steps aside, a little upset, returns to the kitchen. The youngest sits next to the governess, without a word. He doesn't put the cups down right away. He doesn't ask what's wrong.*

*A long silence. The housekeeper always looks out the window.*

**The housekeeper:**
It's difficult, huh?

**The youngest son:**
What then.

**The housekeeper:**
Nothing. One more year. I was looking at the table earlier. There are empty chairs this year.

**The youngest son:**
Yeah.

*He says nothing more. No words about mourning, no comments about the passing generation, nothing that one would usually expect from him. Just silence, aside.*

**The housekeeper:**
Don't you have anything to say for once?

**The youngest son:**
No.

**The housekeeper, with a sad little laugh:**
Gosh.

*The eldest returns with the coffee, stops at a distance upon seeing the scene, does not dare to approach. He looks at his brother, who has still said nothing, who has still done nothing — and yet, visibly, has achieved something that he has not achieved.*

**The housekeeper:**
Stay there a little, you want.

**The youngest son:**
I don't move.

*A silence again, lighter this time. The governess taps the younger brother on the shoulder. They smile at each other.*

*Later, in the kitchen, the eldest puts down the cups, annoyed without really knowing why.*

**The eldest son:**
How did you do it?

**The youngest son:**
I didn't do anything.

**The eldest son:**
Exactly.

**The youngest son:**
Maybe that's the thing.

**The eldest son:**
I suggested a coffee, I even almost suggested going for a walk. I tried everything.

**The youngest son:**
You tried to fix something that just needs to be looked at.

**The eldest son:**
And you just sat down.

**The youngest son:**
Yeah.

**The eldest son:**
This stuff drives me crazy. Usually it's you who explains everything and me who does it. Here it was the opposite and I don't even know what you did.

**The youngest son:**
I didn't explain anything to anyone. Not even to me. I just... didn't need to understand why she was sad. I felt it, there you go!

*The elder looks at him, almost suspicious, as if looking for the theory hidden behind the sentence.*

**The eldest son:**
Are you explaining something to me, or not?

**The youngest son, smiling:**
I don't know. You who see straight away if something works or not — it worked, right?

**The eldest son:**
Yeah. It worked.

**The youngest son:**
So it's good. We don't need to know why.

*The eldest still looks at him, almost incredulous to hear him say that — he who always explains why.*

**The eldest son:**
This is the first time I've heard you say something without justifying it afterwards.

*They look at each other for a second, both almost surprised, then the eldest grabs the two remaining cups of coffee.*

**The eldest son:**
Good. I'll take this to him, then. Without offering anything.

**The youngest son:**
Go ahead.

**The eldest son:**
You will tell me if I did well.

**The youngest son:**
No.

**The eldest son, coming out, half amused:**
Okay.

*The housekeeper stares at him. She stands up, fists on her hips*

**The housekeeper:**
Oh! Are you going to stop going around in circles with this coffee? You've been walking around with it for ten minutes! If you want to drink lukewarm water, there was no point in making a whole pot of coffee!

*The eldest remains silent, his cup in his hand, completely taken aback. On the side, the cadet sketches his little analyst's smile, ready to make a well-felt remark on silent resilience***The housekeeper:**
And the other one who laughs! And, haven't you heard that there hasn't been any music since a while ago! Damn, we're bored here! He plays some tearful songs for us, and what's more, when the song ends, nada, no one moves! Come on, don't worry, I'll take care of it, it'll be great!
