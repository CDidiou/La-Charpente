## What gives way without warning

We got into the habit of climbing slowly. One conversation after another. A partition removed. A chair that we finally leave in its place. A silence that no longer needs to be filled. The childhood home was gradually being transformed into something habitable again. The shutters were new. An old beam had remained visible, deliberately. We even laughed one Sunday while watching the grandchildren banging on the partitions which no longer held anything.

Nobody saw the gesture too much.

They wanted to open a roof window. A strut crossed the place where they wanted to open. The eldest did not want to wait for the craftsman. To him, it was just another piece of wood. He gained a mass. The father intervened, more by reflex than by calculation, because a grandson was too close. The main beam gave way suddenly. No big noise about the end of the world. Just a dull crack, then the weight.

The father found himself on the ground, his legs limp.

At the hospital, the words were simple, almost banal: paralysis. Definitive.

In the first days, no one talked about anything else. We reorganized rooms, learned new words – rehabilitation, chair, caregiver –, signed papers without really reading them. The eldest came every morning before his office opened, leaving late. The younger child came and sat down, without much to say, and stayed longer than expected. The mother commuted, seemingly imperturbable, the toiletry bag always ready in the trunk of the car.

We believed, at that moment, that the worst had already happened — that what happened next would only be a question of time, of adaptation, of quiet courage.

We didn't yet know that it was only the first piece of wood to fall.

The rest, for the moment, still held.

The wood is still there. Broken, but still present. We'll see what we can do with it.
