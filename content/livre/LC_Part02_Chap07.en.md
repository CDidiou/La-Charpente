description: "By trimming the hedge with her son, the mother learns to stop monitoring and making herself useful to others, while the eldest reveals to her that he gets this ability to do things well from her, but that he can now give her back a part of it."

*(A spring afternoon, in the garden. The mother trims a hedge, stops every three gestures to look towards the house — checking a window, listening. The eldest arrives with a second pair of gloves.)*

**The eldest son:**
Here. What are you doing there, turning around all the time?

**The mother:**
I trim, and I make sure your father doesn't put away the barbecue haphazardly.

**The eldest son:**
That's a lot of stuff for a hedge at once.

**The mother:**
It's always like that.

*(He puts on the gloves, takes the remaining pruning shears, begins to cut on the other side, without saying anything at first. She continues to interrupt, look, listen.)*

**The eldest son:**
Mom. Just look at the hedge.

**The mother:**
I look at the hedge.

**The eldest son:**
No, you look at the hedge, the house, the window, and listen at the same time. Just look at the hedge for two minutes.

*(She stops, a little taken aback.)*

**The mother:**
And if there is a problem.

**The eldest son:**
There isn't any. And if there is, I'm here, I move faster than you anyway.

*(She laughs.)*

**The mother:**
Always reassuring me that you're in control.

**The eldest son:**
No. I'm not telling you to trust me. I'm just telling you: for once, you don't have to be the one to check. Cut the branch, see if it's straight, and that's it.

*(She hesitates, then cuts a branch, takes a step back, looks at the result.)*

**The mother:**
That's not bad.

**The eldest son:**
There you go. That's all. Just that.

*(A silence. She cuts again, a little slower, a little more concentrated, without turning towards the house this time.)*

**The mother:**
It's strange. I feel like I'm not doing anything useful here if I'm not watching at the same time.

**The eldest son:**
You're doing something useful. You trim a hedge.

**The mother:**
It's not the same.

**The eldest son:**
Why?

**The mother:**
Because usually, what's useful for me is always for someone. This is just... for the hedge.

*(The eldest stops cutting, looks at her.)*

**The eldest son:**
Do you know where I get it from, this thing of doing something good just to do it well? From you. Except you never did it for yourself. You always did it for us, to make us feel good.

**The mother:**
That's my role.

**The eldest son:**
It was. There, for ten minutes, try something else. Cut a branch just because it's not straight, not because someone needs something.

*(She looks at him, almost destabilized to be caught in the act of something she had never named.)*

**The mother:**
You're teaching me lessons now.

**The eldest son:**
I'm giving you back something you gave me. It's not the same.

*(She smiles, picks up the pruning shears, prunes again, without turning towards the house. The silence that follows is different — denser, calmer.)*

**The mother:**
Your father won't know how to put away this barbecue by himself, mind you.

**The eldest son:**
That's not your problem for ten minutes.

**The mother:**
Okay. Ten minutes.

*(She continues cutting. After a moment, she surprises herself by smiling, alone, in front of a very straight branch, without anyone having seen her do it or without her needing anyone to see it. The eldest, passing behind her to grab the green waste bag, puts his hand on her shoulder for a second - not to reassure her, just to be there - then leaves without a word.)*
